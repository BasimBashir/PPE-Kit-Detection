# Phase 4 log: fall, fight, phone use, stalling (plans, results, what failed)

Decision record: [ADR-015](../decisions/ADR-015-behaviour-checks.md). Every number below is generated by a script
in this repo; per-run JSON next to this file. Dates: 2026-11-23.

**Read this first:** fall and fight are measured on public datasets that differ from Gulf site footage. The numbers
are honest cross-domain numbers and they are **low**. Nothing here supports a customer-facing accuracy claim until
site footage is collected and the models are re-measured (see 7).

## 2. Fall: fast upright -> lying + hold

Method (`app/behaviour/assessors.py: FallAssessor`, ADR-015 §2): torso angle from shoulder/hip keypoints; upright
<= 35 deg, lying >= 60 deg; hips must drop >= 0.25 standing body height within 2 s; the rule's duration policy
(`hold_s = 3.0` in these runs) confirms "still down". `scripts/ml/omnifall_to_csv.py` converts the annotations;
`scripts/ml/eval_fall.py` replays each video through the production pose chain (YOLO pose -> ByteTrack -> assessor).

| run | videos / falls | precision | recall | F1 | false alarms / h | confirmable falls caught | onset detected |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| OmniFall ItW @ 5 fps (`fall_eval_itw_fps5.json`) | 572 / 829 | 0.67 | 0.005 | 0.010 | 1.09 | 3 / 461 = 0.65 % | 144 / 829 = 17.4 % |
| OmniFall ItW @ 10 fps (`fall_eval_itw_fps10.json`) | 572 / 829 | 0.80 | 0.010 | 0.019 | 1.09 | 7 / 461 = 1.5 % | 229 / 829 = 27.6 % |
| OmniFall Syn @ 5 fps (`fall_eval_syn_fps5.json`) | 1,200 / 212 | 0.89 | 0.038 | 0.072 | 0.60 | 2 / 125 = 1.6 % | 43 / 212 = 20.3 % |

- **Missed while lying:** 390 (ItW@5), 448 (ItW@10), 140 (Syn@5) falls where the person was visibly lying but no
  event was raised - the assessor rarely reaches the "down after a fall" state.
- **The transition is detected far more often than it is confirmed**, and the gap is the signal: the upright sample
  within the 2 s window (or the hip drop check) fails on wild footage, where the pose model loses the torso at the
  moment of the fall (motion blur, camera cut, crowd occlusion).
- For reference, OmniFall's own paper reports ~65.6 F1 for its best wild-set model with per-dataset training. Our
  number is a **zero-shot** result on footage the pose model was never tuned for; the honest conclusion is that
  fall detection needs site footage and probably a pose model trained/fine-tuned on CCTV angles.
- What the design already prevents: slow lying down, bending and squatting never fire (unit tests
  `tests/unit/test_behaviour.py`); a lying person without a fast transition is `horizontal_without_fall`, not a fall.

## 3. Fight: pair-keypoint GRU on CPU

Method (`app/behaviour/fight.py`, ADR-015 §5): people closer than 1.2 mean body heights form a pair; features are
2 x 17 keypoints pair-centred and body-height normalised (102 floats x 8 samples = 1.4 s @ 5 Hz); the same
`pair_features`/`PairSequencer` code builds training data (`scripts/ml/extract_fight_sequences.py`) and serves
(`FightAssessor`). Training: `scripts/ml/train_fight.py` (40 epochs, clip-level split, no leakage).

| run | clips | threshold | precision | recall | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RLVS val (in-domain, `val:rlvs`) | 400 | 0.50 | 0.78 | 0.56 | 0.65 |
| RLVS val, best threshold | 400 | 0.55 | 0.79 | 0.56 | **0.65** |
| SCFD cross-dataset (`test:scfd`) | 300 | 0.50 | 0.54 | 0.31 | **0.39** |
| SCFD at the rule default | 300 | 0.70 | 0.56 | 0.29 | 0.38 |

- In-domain the GRU is usable as an MVP; cross-dataset it loses ~40 % of its F1, the usual skeleton-action domain
  gap. For reference, RGB action models report ~93 % (in-dataset, RWF-2000) and skeleton methods ~83-88 %
  cross-dataset - both on curated clip sets, not CCTV.
- Weights `fight_gru.pt` (`fight_gru@b72ce7e0d017`) are **MVP weights**: retrain on licensed or client footage
  before commercial deployment (licences, section 1 above).
- Default rule threshold 0.7 was kept: on SCFD it trades almost no recall for fewer false positives.

## 4. Phone: COCO class 67 quality at the deployed input sizes

Method (`app/behaviour/assessors.py: PhoneAssessor`; detector stage in `app/inference/ultralytics_detector.py`):
a COCO detector produces `phone` detections; a phone box overlapping a 1.8x grown hand or head ROI with IoA >= 0.3
is evidence; dropouts shorter than 2 s are bridged. Eval: `scripts/ml/eval_phone_coco.py` on COCO val2017 class 67.

| weights | imgsz | precision | recall | AP50 | AP50-95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `yolo26s.pt` | 640 | 0.644 | 0.569 | **0.598** | 0.413 |
| `yolo26s.pt` | 1280 | 0.573 | 0.515 | **0.547** | 0.361 |

- **This is a proxy, not the field number.** COCO phones are close-up, large, well-lit; a phone at CCTV distance is
  a handful of pixels. 1280 is *worse* than 640 on COCO (upscaling close-ups adds nothing) - it is kept as the
  deployed size because distant phones need the pixels; the real number must come from site footage.
- The per-rule shift schedule matters here: phone detections outside work hours are not events (`schedule`).

## 5. Stalling: stillness, no accuracy claim

Method (`IdleAssessor`): every keypoint and the box centre must stay within 0.08 body heights (5th-95th percentile
over a 10 s window). **No public ground truth for stalling exists**, so only timer correctness is tested
(warming-up, still vs moving, history pruning: `tests/unit/test_behaviour.py`). It measures stillness, not intent:
scope the rule to a work-area zone and a shift schedule. No precision/recall is claimed.

## 6. GPU / CPU cost of Phase 4 (10 cameras, 4K, construction preset + 4 behaviour rules + phone stage)

`scripts/benchmark/run_benchmark.py --profile p4_uhd_cams10_fps5_nvdec` (10 x 3840x2160 H.264 25 fps through the RTSP
simulator; RTX 3090; 60 s measured, 25 s warmup). Phase 3 baseline in the same table.

| run | processed fps | frame age p95 | GPU % mean/max | host CPU % | VRAM GB | worker RSS GB | assoc ms p50 | total ms p50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| p3_uhd_cams10_fps5_nvdec (3 models, 5 PPE rules) | **20.7** / 50 | 0.14 s | 31 / 69 | 57 | 10.4 | 6.8 | 0.70 | 48.5 |
| p4_uhd_cams10_fps5_nvdec (4 models, 5 PPE + 4 behaviour rules) | **13.8** / 50 | 0.16 s | 31 / 74 | 59 | 9.9 | 7.1 | **22.2** | 76.5 |

- The whole delta is in **association** (0.7 -> 22 ms p50), i.e. the CPU assessors on the inference thread, not the
  GPU: the phone stage adds ~8 ms p50 of GPU time, the three CNN stages are unchanged.
- Cause, measured directly: `FightAssessor` pair sequencing is quadratic in the number of close people -
  1.0 ms/frame at 4 people, 5.2 at 10, **16.0 at 20** (`PairSequencer.update` 29.5 ms for 135 pairs; the GRU score
  itself is 1.2 ms). Vectorising `_sequence` without changing features is the next step (ADR-015 follow-ups).
- `IdleAssessor` recomputed a 10 s percentile window per person per frame (~1.5 ms each); it now caches the result
  at 2 Hz (0.04 ms/call for 6 people). It did not move the benchmark (fight dominates) but removes a fixed cost in
  crowded scenes.
- Deployment sizing: the Phase 3 rule "one inference worker ~= 20 processed 4K fps" becomes **~14 with all four
  behaviour rules on** until the pair sequencer is vectorised. Phone/fight/stalling are per-rule, so disabling
  them restores the Phase 3 number.

## 7. Not measured / rejected

- **Site footage:** not available. Fall/fight/phone field accuracy: **not measured**; models are MVP.
- **Stalling accuracy:** no ground truth exists; not measured by design.
- **Emotion ("angry"):** dropped by the user; emotion models are ~59-61 % accurate on close-up faces (AffectNet),
  worse at CCTV distance.
- **"Not working":** undefined observable; dropped.
- **Generic accident detection:** parked until a client proposal.
- **RGB video transformer for fights:** not tried; GPU cost on the inference path, no measured need yet.
- **Track stitching:** deferred; the fall misses are dominated by pose quality/domain shift, not ID switches.
- **Teacher review of behaviours:** not built; the rubric is PPE-only.

## 8. Live end-to-end verification (2026-11-24)

The classes were also fired **inside the running app**, not only in the offline replays: real test clips published
as camera streams through the demo RTSP path (MediaMTX), per-camera behaviour rules created in the Rules UI,

- **fall** (`fall-cam01` on cam01, OmniFall syn `fall_to_161`): PERSON DOWN confirmed - 5 of 5 decisive observations,
  mean confidence 0.99, first seen -> confirmed 3.21 s, evidence snapshot persisted with the banner drawn in.
- **fight** (`fight-cam02` on cam02, SCFD test `fi003`): FIGHT confirmed - 3 decisive votes, evidence snapshot
  persisted, and the event's model list carries `fight: fight_gru@<sha>`.

Both took the production path (assessor -> state machine -> event -> evidence -> dashboard) with zero worker errors.
Phone and stalling were not fired live: no real phone-use video exists (section 1) and stalling's production hold is
600 s - their proof stays the synthetic demos and timer tests.

Operational notes:

- **Fight weights are off by default** (`fight_weights` is commented out in `models.example.yaml`): without it fight
  rules stay UNCERTAIN by design. Enabling it in a local `configs/models.yaml` loads `models/fight_gru.pt` and fight
  rules start confirming; the MVP-weights caveat in section 7 still applies.
- The raw OmniFall clips break `ffmpeg -stream_loop` (broken fps metadata; OpenCV reports fps = -1 - the same reason
  the demo renderer derives fps from duration). Re-encode before looping one as a camera stream
  (`ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -r 25 -pix_fmt yuv420p out.mp4`); otherwise the stream publisher crash-loops (77 restarts
  observed; the app's backoff and OFFLINE health state handled it as designed and recovered after the re-encode).
